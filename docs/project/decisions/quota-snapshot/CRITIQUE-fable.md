**Verdict in three sentences.** The document is right about the shape of history (a series of immutable, versioned facts is what Ruling 6 has been asking for) and wrong about what it costs: its two headline claims — "not an engine rewrite" (DESIGN:18) and the dissolution table (DESIGN:27-33) — are respectively false and half-true, and the half that is not true is *relocated into a component the design declares permanently out of scope* (DESIGN:242-245). Separately, the draft bundles two independent moves — (1) the scheduler consumes a resolved, versioned, validated document instead of deriving identity from raw Budgets, and (2) authority leaves Kubernetes for an external control plane — and sells them as one purchase, when every dissolution in its own table comes from move (1) alone. Move (2) is a values choice about where authoring should live, and it should be put to the owner as that, with its bill attached.

## 1. The dissolution claim, tested finding by finding

| Finding | Draft's claim | My verdict |
|---|---|---|
| F2 unrooted squatter | "no cluster-wide aggregation left to poison" (DESIGN:29) | **Half dissolved, half relocated — and the relocated half has no invariant, no test, and no owner.** |
| F3 interior exemption | "bindings are stated, not derived" (DESIGN:30) | **Dissolved**, at the price of a blast-radius inversion nobody has signed off (§1c below). |
| F1 replay rewrite | "each accrual interval anchors to the snapshot version in effect during it" (DESIGN:31) | **Relocated from a pinned evaluator bug into an unbuilt evaluator feature.** True in the model; enforced by nothing that exists or is specified. |
| F5 compaction ≠ immutability | keyed by `(envelopeKey, snapshotVersion)` (DESIGN:118-121) | **Correctly specified; entirely dependent on P3**, which `DECISIONS-NEEDED.md:22` still classifies as "a feature deferral, not correctness" on main. |
| P6 deletion blindness | "absent from version N+1 while version N still carries it" (DESIGN:33) | **Conditional on the same two unbuilt things as F1 and F5** (versioned replay + P3-bounded retention). |

### 1a. F2 — the containment property leaves the verification perimeter

Today F2 is a bug in a function this repo owns, with a compiled known-bad specimen (`TestUnrootedSquatterChangesVictimBinding`, `pkg/funding/grant_authority_counterexample_test.go:15` on main) and a proposed machine-checked rail (`INV-GRANT-LOCAL`, FINAL-RECOMMENDATION §P5). Under the draft, the scheduler-side attack surface genuinely disappears: `deriveOwners` (`evaluate.go:206-272`) is deleted, there is no scan to poison, and `INV-ROOTED` (DESIGN:154-155) rejects an unrooted principal at the door.

But `INV-ROOTED` checks that the *document* is internally rooted. It cannot check that the *write that produced the document* was authorized. A compromised, buggy, or merely confused producer can emit a perfectly rooted, perfectly injective snapshot in which the victim's namespace UID is bound to the attacker's principal — every §4 invariant passes. The property Ruling 1 actually requires — "a hostile writer at any level must be *contained to what they were granted*" (`OWNER-RULINGS.md:20-21`) — is now a property of the producer's authorization logic. And the draft's §8 places exactly that logic outside the design, permanently: "validation that a grant was *authorised* by its grantor" (DESIGN:244-245). `provenanceRef` does not help; the draft says so itself — "Blame lives in Postgres" (DESIGN:96) — and blame is forensics, not containment.

So the coordinator's worry is confirmed for F2, precisely: the defect is not dissolved, it is moved to a control plane where nobody is checking — where *nothing in this repo can check*, because the TLA models, the envtest specimens, the invariant oracle (`World` is `Runs`+`Leases` only, `pkg/invariant/invariant.go:230-233`), and all five compiled counterexamples speak Kubernetes objects. The one thing that would make this honest is a sentence the draft does not contain: **the producer's write-authorization is a correctness dependency of this design, it must have its own invariant (the snapshot-level restatement of `INV-GRANT-LOCAL`: a changeset authored by principal P may alter bindings and envelopes only within P's subtree), and until a producer exists that enforces it, F2 is open — not closed — under this design.**

### 1b. F1 — "by construction" is a claim about code nobody has priced

The draft's mechanism is right: anchor each accrual interval to the contemporaneous snapshot version. But walk the code it claims is already snapshot-shaped:

- `Input` carries exactly **one** `Budgets` slice (`evaluate.go:29`).
- The graph and the bindings are built **once per call** (`evaluate.go:322-333`), and every claim's tier comes from that one graph (`evaluate.go:707`).
- `windowActive` consults `acct.Spec` — the current spec — at **every replay instant** (`evaluate.go:634-642`), and `accrue` clamps to the current `MaxGPUHours` (`evaluate.go:993-997`).

To anchor intervals to their versions, the replay loop must swap the *entire configuration surface* — envelope index, family graph, namespace bindings — at snapshot boundaries: `eventTimes` must ingest version `effectiveFrom`s (`evaluate.go:572-600`), `fill` must resolve tier and window against the segment's version (`evaluate.go:675-831`), `nextDepletion` must handle an integral that is per-window rather than per-envelope (`evaluate.go:929-970`), and `EnvelopeAccount` must stop assuming one `Spec` per key (`evaluate.go:93-115`). This is, line for line, the risk FINAL-RECOMMENDATION already flagged as its uncertainty #5 ("two integrals and one loop"). The draft's response is DESIGN:18: "this is not an engine rewrite. It is a change to who fills `Input`." That sentence is false, and it contradicts the draft's own §2, whose semantics cannot be produced by any filling of an `Input` that holds one spec.

Worse, the invariant that is supposed to catch a lazy implementation — `INV-ACCRUAL-PREFIX-IMMUTABLE` "exists to catch an implementation that reaches for the current snapshot instead of the effective one" (DESIGN:186-188) — has no stated checker. The invariant oracle cannot see snapshots. The formal campaign explicitly could not build the temporal differential test "because production exposes neither a persisted prefix nor an effective mutation timestamp" (formal results, Accrual prefix section). The snapshot design *supplies* the missing timestamp (`effectiveFrom`) — which means the previously unwritable test is now writable — and then does not require it. In a repo that names comment-as-enforcement as a defect class in the very file under discussion (`evaluate.go:176-182`), an invariant with no assigned oracle is a known failure mode, not an oversight to forgive.

**And the specimens.** When `deriveOwners` is deleted, `TestUnrootedSquatterChangesVictimBinding`, `TestInteriorExemptionAllowsOwnedChargeAcrossNamespaces` (`grant_authority_counterexample_test.go:15,50`), and both accrual-prefix specimens (`accrual_prefix_counterexample_test.go:62,82`) do not turn green — they stop compiling and will be deleted. The draft names no successors. The minimum honest set: loader-reject conformance tests for every §4 invariant, a producer-authorization specimen (the F2 restatement above), and the versioned-replay differential (burn 40 hours under version N, publish N+1 delaying `Start`; assert the 40 stay charged). Without that list in the design, the exact thing the coordinator feared happens by default: the invariant that caught the defect no longer runs.

### 1c. F3 — dissolved, but the failure containment inverts

Real dissolution: injectivity becomes a data property (`INV-BINDING-INJECTIVE`, DESIGN:151-153), checked at load, with `INV-OWNED-IS-LOCAL` retained as the end-to-end rail (DESIGN:189-192). Good. But note what the remedy costs. Today an injectivity conflict fails *the touched namespaces* safe and the rest of the cluster proceeds (`evaluate.go:253-271`). Under the draft, the same conflict anywhere in the document ⇒ **reject the whole document** (DESIGN:132-137). One team's collision now freezes *every* tenant's quota updates at last-good — including a director's revocation that happened to be in the same rejected version, which means **a rejected snapshot silently extends the life of every grant the next version was going to revoke**, for as long as the producer takes to fix somebody else's error. The `staleMax` bound (DESIGN:205) technically covers this, but §6 frames staleness as a control-plane *outage*; validation rejection is self-inflicted staleness with the control plane perfectly healthy, and the draft never prices it. Whole-document semantics versus per-namespace containment is a genuine design choice with a loser either way — it should be surfaced to the owner, not inherited from Envoy by analogy.

### 1d. Two consumers, one version — the coherence gap nobody mentions

The scheduler *plugin* is a second, out-of-process consumer of identity: `cmd/scheduler/plugin/gang.go:795` calls `funding.OwnerOfNamespace(budgetList.Items, ...)`, and that function exists precisely "so the two can never drift into disagreeing about who pays" (`evaluate.go:296-308`). Under the draft, controller and plugin each poll the control plane independently; §3's atomic swap is per-process and synchronizes nothing across them. At a mint instant the controller can hold version N and the plugin N+1, and the anti-drift rail the comment describes quietly dissolves. The fix is knowable now and absent from the schema: **the promise/lease must record the `snapshotVersion` it was minted under, and the plugin must validate provenance against that version** — which also happens to be the record the versioned replay needs anyway. Its absence from §1 is a schema defect, not a nicety.

### 1e. `effectiveFrom` reopens F1 at the seam

§3 step 2 requires `snapshotVersion` strictly increasing and `effectiveFrom` non-decreasing (DESIGN:130-131). Nothing requires `effectiveFrom` to be *at or after the scheduler's receipt of the version*. A producer that publishes version N+1 with an `effectiveFrom` in the past retroactively reassigns intervals already attributed (in every recomputation since — Decision 3 recomputes on demand) to version N. That is the F1 rewrite wearing the new design's own clothes, and `INV-SNAP-MONOTONE` does not catch it. Required: the scheduler anchors each interval at `max(effectiveFrom, firstSeen(version))`, or the loader rejects an `effectiveFrom` earlier than the predecessor's receipt. One line in §4; without it the whole "in effect during" edifice trusts the producer's clock discipline, which is another relocation.

## 2. The operational bill, walked at 3am

The project's ethos is fail-closed and self-contained. This design puts an external store on the funding path and then mitigates it. Every row of §6's table is a failure mode the design introduces and then manages. Price the management:

**The alarm surface is aspirational.** The draft says "alarmed" or "alarm" six times (DESIGN:137, 146-150, 201-208). In this codebase, the existing binding-conflict alarm channel has had zero production consumers since R26 — `Conflicts()` has no non-test caller on main (verified: `git grep "\.Conflicts()"` hits tests only; `evaluate.go:176-182` says so in prose). Credit where due: the *reservation* side did get real since the recommendation was written — `BlockedFunding` with a durable `BlockedSince` landed (`api/v1/reservation_types.go:68-71`) and the reconciler drives it (`controllers/kube/reconcilers.go:375-392`), so "blocked is visible" is no longer vapor. But the design's *new* alarms (rejection-naming-the-invariant, `staleWarn`, `INV-SNAP-IMMUTABLE` "loudly") name no mechanism, no metric, no event, no page. Until the design says *what emits them and what consumes them*, the fail-closed contract is: real validation, imaginary observability.

**The 3am walk.** Researcher's job won't schedule. Operator runs — what? There is no stated surface for "current snapshot version / age / last rejection" on any object an operator can `kubectl get`. If Q2 resolves to deleting the Budget CRD, `kubectl` shows *nothing about quota at all*. The current tenant-facing message explains Unfunded in terms of Budgets ("it has no Budget, or its Budgets name more than one owner", `controllers/run_controller.go:1279`) — under this design that sentence becomes false and every message like it must be rewritten in terms of an artifact tenants cannot inspect. Diagnosis then goes: scheduler log → version number → `provenanceRef` → `pg://quota/changesets/8871` → a Postgres instance the cluster operator may not own, with a schema this repo does not define. The design has **no break-glass**: no documented way to hand-place a snapshot when the control plane is down and a director is standing behind you. (Q3's static-file producer is the germ of one; it is listed as an option, and it is actually a requirement — see §7.)

**Cold start is right — say so.** "Fund nothing" (DESIGN:206, 210-212) survives scrutiny *because of Ruling 4*: with nothing funded, no funded demand exists, `Resolve` returns on non-positive deficit (`resolver.go:74-75`), so nothing is evicted; running work coasts, new work queues. The draft chose the correct corner and the mechanism already enforces it. Two residues: (a) `staleMax` option (b) (DESIGN:221-226) is ambiguous about *running* work's class — does it keep its stale classification (a revoked tenant's work stays Owned and lottery-immune through the whole outage) or demote (which is option (c) for everything running)? The two readings put opposite tenants in front of the resolver; the owner cannot choose (b) until (b) says which it is. (b) The "local durability" cache "(or a ConfigMap)" (DESIGN:214-215): the retention floor is keyed to `SettlementHorizon` (DESIGN:112-114), P3 does not exist, so pre-P3 the series reaches back to the oldest retained lease's start — for this project's target workload that is *months of training runs*, hundreds of versions, which is not ConfigMap arithmetic (1MiB) and multiplies the cost of every `Evaluate` call by the version count in the window. The draft mentions that P3 "releases snapshots" (DESIGN:115-117); it does not say the load-bearing converse: **until P3 lands, this design's memory and compute are unbounded by anything but lease GC, and P3 is still classified on main as optional** (`DECISIONS-NEEDED.md:22`). P3 must be promoted to a named prerequisite of this design, in this design.

## 3. What the snapshot cannot express — the F4 walk the brief asked for

Can the scheduler compute, from this document plus live leases, whether a subtree exceeds its ancestor's allocation, in both dimensions, with loans crossing sideways?

**Lineage: yes.** `children` edges plus `INV-ROOTED`/`INV-ACYCLIC` give every payer a unique root path — this is genuinely new and valuable, because F4's formal finding was exactly that "no production grant trace exists to implement" payer-lineage conservation. The snapshot *is* that trace. Credit.

**The lease→principal join: no — a field is missing.** The predicate is keyed on the *payer's* position (FINAL-RECOMMENDATION §3.3, and the draft adopts it, DESIGN:181-186). Live leases identify their payer as `{PaidByBudgetNamespace, PaidByBudget, PaidByEnvelope}` — a namespace *name* plus a *Budget object name* (`evaluate.go:692,984`; `funding.go:174-183`). The snapshot's envelope identity is `(owner, envelope.name)` with **no Budget level at all** (§1 schema; `INV-ENVELOPE-UNIQUE`, DESIGN:162), and its namespace identity is a **UID**, deliberately (DESIGN:62, 84-86). So the scheduler cannot join a lease to a principal without either (a) the lease recording the payer *principal id* (and, per §1d above, the mint-time `snapshotVersion`), or (b) a name→UID→owner mapping that quietly re-imports the recreated-namespace ambiguity the UID field exists to kill. This is a lease-schema migration — the class of change R7 already schedules as an outage — and the draft never mentions it. **Name the field: leases must carry `paidByPrincipal` (+ mint `snapshotVersion`), or the design's central predicate is uncomputable.**

**Concurrency dimension: computable** once the join exists — sum funded width whose payer lineage traverses P against P's window-active envelope concurrency per flavor. But note the enforcement gap the draft inherits without saying so: *enforcing* (not just checking) it means the greedy fill stops being "against the envelope's concurrency and remaining integral" and becomes fill against a **path of caps** — an amendment to the normative core of binding text (`quota-semantics.md:71-81`), named by FINAL-RECOMMENDATION §3.3 as "the largest piece of unscheduled work in this recommendation". The snapshot changes none of that arithmetic. F4 stays in the scheduler, and stays unbuilt, and the draft's §5 reads as if stating `INV-SUBTREE-CONSERVE` were most of the job.

**Hours dimension: not computable, and the draft asserts it anyway.** DESIGN:181-183 states the invariant over "windowed GPU-hours, separately". The formal campaign explicitly parked this on an owner decision — misaligned ancestor/descendant windows force a choice between intersect-with-ancestor-window and attribute-whole-epoch, "each yields a materially different tenant outcome... Until the owner chooses, `INV-SUBTREE-CONSERVE` is checked only for instantaneous concurrency" (formal results, Design decisions required). The schema carries nothing that resolves this, and §7's open questions (Q1-Q4) do not list it. A design that silently asserts what a binding campaign explicitly parked is manufacturing false consensus. Either list it as Q5 or scope the invariant to concurrency.

**Schema completeness, while we are here.** The §1 envelope drops three Budget-side features that exist on main with real consumers: `AggregateCaps` (`api/v1/budget_types.go:35`; enforced at `evaluate.go:354-373,862-868`; also load-bearing for compaction — `settlementSafe` refuses aggregate-capped budgets at `evaluate.go:507`, so their omission or inclusion interacts with §2's settlement story either way), `PreActivation` (`budget_types.go:72`; read by cover/forecast; it is the mechanism behind the draft's own fail-closed pre-window admission stance), and `AutoRenew`/`PendingRenewals` (`budget_types.go:37`; the only renewal-notification hook, `quota-semantics.md:141-147`). Perhaps all three deliberately become control-plane concerns — fine — but a schema that silently omits enforced features is a migration trap, and `AGENTS.md`'s no-side-by-side-compat rule means each omission is a scheduled breaking change someone must own. Also missing from §4: uniqueness of `principals[].owner` itself (asserted in a comment at DESIGN:59, absent from the invariant list, and `INV-REFS-RESOLVE` is ill-defined without it).

## 4. The grandparent problem: fixed for identity, dodged for capacity

Direct answer: **the snapshot fixes the authority chain at any depth and silently inherits the four-tier capacity ceiling.**

- Identity: `children` edges compose to arbitrary depth; `INV-ROOTED` walks them; a root→manager→lead→researcher chain is expressible and validated. Ruling 1's shape is served. Genuine fix.
- Capacity: nothing in the draft touches the tier function. `Tier` returns owner/child/sibling/cousin and otherwise `tierNone` (`funding.go:132-150` — verified: for the four-level chain, `Tier(manager, researcher)` falls through every branch to `tierNone`). The word "tier" does not appear in DESIGN.md. So under the snapshot, exactly as today: a researcher's run cannot draw the manager's slack as family (`tierNone` ⇒ `sponsored`, `evaluate.go:707-708`) unless the manager writes a **lending policy to their own grandchild** — Decision 2's "within the family, no gates" is false for the family Ruling 1 describes, and the pass-through workaround still "bounds one level only" (FINAL-RECOMMENDATION §3.3). The draft's own §1 example is a two-level tree, which is the only depth at which the schema and the engine agree.

One more silent narrowing: `INV-ACYCLIC` requires **at most one parent** (DESIGN:156-158). Today `Spec.Parents` is a list and `NewFamilyGraph` builds a DAG (`budget_types.go:36`; `funding.go:60-73`), and the sibling/cousin machinery handles multi-parent. The single-parent rule is defensible (the draft's conservation argument is sound) but it is an amendment to the binding family model, not a validation detail — see §6 on the missing amendments section.

## 5. Migration and the human test

**Q2 (Budget CRD survives?):** neither branch is priced honestly yet.

- *Read-only projection:* costs a controller **plus a freshness invariant**. A drifting read-only projection is the frozen-backlog-gauge defect in CRD form — state wrong and the surface lying about it — and this project has already ruled that class intolerable. If the projection exists, `INV-PROJECTION-MATCHES-SNAPSHOT` (version + hash stamped on the projected object) is mandatory, and that is new machinery the draft doesn't count.
- *Delete it:* every tenant loses `kubectl` visibility into their own quota (`BudgetStatus.Headroom/Usage` is today's self-service answer to "why is my job Unfunded"); the R18 runbook, the R23 CLI, the R11 conditions, and tenant-facing message text (`run_controller.go:1279`) all reference Budgets; and namespaced RBAC — the thing that today scopes who may *see* quota — has no analogue in the control plane until someone builds one.

**The human test the draft omits entirely.** The prior recommendation carried a §7 answering "what does a tenant type; what does a lead type." This draft has no such section, and the answers under it are: tenant — `kubectl describe run` (works, if the message text is rewritten) then a dead end at `provenanceRef` unless they hold a control-plane account; lead granting a researcher quota — an API call to a service that does not exist, authenticated by a mechanism that does not exist, authorized by a delegation ACL that does not exist. For a kubectl-native shop the honest sentence is: **the entire delegation UX moves from "kubectl apply, gated by RBAC you already run" to "a web app or CLI against a new service with its own accounts."** That may be the right trade — directors are not kubectl users — but it is a cost with a name, and the draft's §8 lists it as a feature ("authoring and the delegation UX" stays outside) without listing the build.

And note what came back through the window: the P5 exchange's unresolved Disagreement 1 dinged the cluster-registry option because "the registry therefore needs its own rooted authorization mechanism. At that point it is a delegated grant registry" (Sol's concession, quoted at FINAL-RECOMMENDATION §4). The snapshot control plane **is that registry, minus etcd** — every objection transfers intact, now without even Kubernetes RBAC primitives to lean on. The draft supersedes R1 without answering it; it re-houses it.

## 7. The strongest case for keeping authority in Kubernetes

I can make it strong, and here it is at full strength.

1. **The authorization machinery already exists there, and it is the whole problem.** The single hardest unsolved question in this domain — who may write a grant, bounded to their subtree — is solved in-cluster by namespaced RBAC plus grantor-side grants with zero new machinery (Disagreement 1, Fable's strongest form; Sol conceded the write-boundary point at E:46). Outside the cluster you rebuild authentication, delegated authorization, and audit from nothing, on the funding path, and that rebuild *is* F2's containment property. The design moves the load-bearing security property from a place with mature primitives to a place with none.
2. **Fate-sharing beats managed staleness.** Authority in etcd cannot be stale relative to the cluster it governs: etcd down means no scheduling anyway; there is no `staleWarn`, no `staleMax`, no cold-start rule to choose, because the failure domain is shared. Every row of §6's fail-closed table is a failure mode the exodus creates and then mitigates. A self-contained project should need overwhelming cause to buy a second availability domain for its most safety-critical input.
3. **The verification perimeter.** This project's differentiator is that its funding semantics are pinned by TLC, Apalache, envtest, compiled counterexamples, and an invariant oracle — all of which read Kubernetes objects. Moving authority out moves the identity plane beyond the reach of every rail the last two months built. §1a is not an implementation gap; it is the structural consequence.
4. **Containment granularity.** Per-object reconciliation fails one namespace at a time (`evaluate.go:253-271`); whole-document semantics fails everyone's updates at once (§1c).
5. **The dissolutions do not require the exodus.** This is the decisive point. F1/F5/P6 are closed by *versioned, effective-dated, immutable history* — Ruling 6's own analysis says the mechanism is P3, which is location-independent. F2/F3 are closed by *stated-not-derived bindings validated against a rooted trace* — which an **in-cluster producer** (a controller compiling Budgets+grants into a cluster-scoped, versioned, hash-chained `QuotaSnapshot` object, whole-document semantics included) provides while keeping kubectl, RBAC, GitOps, air-gap, and the oracle perimeter intact. Even the pointer-direction insulation (DESIGN:248-250) survives: the scheduler reads the compiled document, not the authored edges. What the external control plane adds *beyond* that synthesis is exactly two things: an authoring UX for non-kubectl humans, and Postgres-native provenance. Both are real; neither is a correctness property; and etcd document-size limits (~1.5MiB) are the one genuine scale ceiling of the in-cluster variant — name it and size it against the actual org tree before treating it as decisive.

Because move (1) — the document — captures the entire dissolution table, and move (2) — leaving Kubernetes — captures the entire §2/§5 cost table, the draft's framing ("the authority record leaves Kubernetes entirely", DESIGN:4-5) is not the conservative reading of its own evidence. If the owner wants the exodus, it should be because he wants authoring outside the cluster *as such*, with the bill visible — not because the dissolutions appeared to require it.

Corollary for Q3: the in-tree producer is not an open question. Without it there is no component in this repo that *produces* documents, so every §4 invariant is enforced only against artifacts nobody here can generate, and none of it is testable in CI. The reference producer (static file → validate → publish) is the prerequisite that makes the design executable at all, air-gap coverage included. Q4 (poll) is fine; polling matches the staleness math and nothing in quota needs xDS latency.

## 8. Smaller defects, each real

1. **`overAllocatedBy` has no home** (DESIGN:168-169). It is "surfaced as status" — on what object? If the Budget CRD dies, Ruling 3's "you're over by" counter has no Kubernetes surface; if the projection lives, say so and bind the field. Also, computing it at all is §3's path-of-caps work; per FINAL-RECOMMENDATION §7, if that is not built the field must be *absent*, never zero.
2. **`INV-SNAP-IMMUTABLE` is only enforceable over retained versions.** Once the scheduler releases a version past the horizon, it has no memory of its hash; republication of ancient history is detectable only by Postgres — trust relocated again. And if the local cache is a ConfigMap, every ConfigMap-writer becomes a potential history rewriter the scheduler will trust on restart. Prefer scheduler-local disk with the scheduler as sole writer, or hash-chain the series so version N's hash commits to N-1's.
3. **`sharing: "Family"`** (DESIGN:73) is a new enum; today's values are `""`/`none` (`funding.go:237-239`). Trivial, but the schema should use the vocabulary the engine validates or declare the mapping.
4. **The design cites Sol's UID point correctly** (`E-sol-responds-to-fable.md:25`) — verified — but adopts only half of E:25-28's package: "identity and allocation must remain logically distinct inside the grant" is honored (principals with no envelopes are legal, DESIGN:171-174 — good), while roots-as-exact-tuples is weakened to bare principal ids (`"roots": ["org:root"]`, DESIGN:55) with no namespace binding required at the root. Probably fine since roots need not bind namespaces at all here; state it.
5. **Terminology drift:** DESIGN:207 says a payer-absent lease "coasts, per `quota-semantics.md:27`". Mechanically true today (`acct == nil` ⇒ Unfunded, `evaluate.go:694-698`), but remember coast is the resolver's *first* reclaim target (`evaluate.go:280-286`); under a snapshot mistake (producer drops a principal), the whole victim org's running work becomes first-cut simultaneously. A one-version rollback path in the loader ("pin to version N") is the operator lever that turns this from an incident into a blip; the design has no rollback verb at all — versions only go forward (`INV-SNAP-MONOTONE`), so the only repair for a bad-but-valid snapshot is publishing N+2, through the same producer that just published the bad one, possibly at 3am. Give the operator a scheduler-side pin.

## 9. Where the draft is right — briefly, so the record is fair

Stated bindings over derived ones; UID-not-name; whole-document atomic swap with NACK semantics; over-allocation as legal, loadable data (exactly Ruling 2); principal-without-envelopes making the GitOps window benign (exactly the recommendation's P5 clause 5); series-not-document as the headline constraint (exactly Ruling 6, and better than either designer's P6 proposal); `(envelopeKey, snapshotVersion)` settled-accrual keying (anticipates FINAL-RECOMMENDATION §3.8c precisely); cold-start fund-nothing; and the pointer-flip test at DESIGN:248-250, which is a genuinely good acceptance criterion. None of these needed defending; the draft's problems are in what it prices, not what it wants.

## 10. Values questions for David — stated, not answered

1. **Severability.** Every dissolution in the draft's table is delivered by the *document* (resolved bindings, versioned immutable history, load-time validation); every new cost is delivered by the *exodus* (external store, bespoke auth, staleness contract, migration, verification blindness). An in-cluster producer writing the same versioned document buys the first list without the second. Do you want quota authoring outside Kubernetes *because the cluster is the wrong place for directors and their tooling to live* — a legitimate position — or did the Envoy analogy make one move look like two? (Note the analogy's limit: Envoy's routes are stateless config; quota is a ledger, and the snapshot *series* + retention + versioned replay is exactly the machinery Envoy never needed.)
2. **Failure containment.** When one team's grant collides, today their two namespaces stop funding and everyone else proceeds; under whole-document rejection, everyone's updates freeze — including pending revocations — until the producer repairs. Which do you want a tenant to experience: localized fail-safe with cluster-wide inconsistency possible, or global consistency with cluster-wide update outages possible? This is a containment-versus-consistency value, and the draft currently chooses consistency silently.

**Bottom line for the coordinator.** Adopt the document shape and the series semantics; refuse the "smaller than it sounds" framing until the versioned replay is priced as the engine change it is; require the design to name P3, the in-tree producer, the producer-authorization invariant, the lease `paidByPrincipal`/`snapshotVersion` fields, the specimen successors, and its amendments to binding text as prerequisites in its own §7; and put questions 1 and 2 above to the owner *before* Q1-Q4, because their answers decide whether Q2-Q4 are even the right questions.
