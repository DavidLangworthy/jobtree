## 2. The legitimacy argument — Sol is right, and the asymmetries I declined

Conceded plainly: universal injectivity detects a collision and cannot select legitimacy. Two Budgets carrying the same owner string are a symmetric input to `deriveOwners` (evaluate.go:206-272); the evaluator can poison both (:243-251, :256-261) or pick arbitrarily, and I chose symmetric poisoning. That stops the cross-namespace Owned charge and preserves remote denial — W-delayed, alarmed (once R26 is actually wired; evaluate.go:176-182 says nothing consumes `Conflicts()` yet), non-destructive, but inducible by any Budget writer. Sol's statement of this (C:40-57) is correct and my A-position never addressed it.

The asymmetries available to a scan, which I used silently and should have named:

- **Creation order.** Against an incumbent the attacker always loses the race — the victim's Budget predates the squatter by definition. But squat-first inverts it: a Budget carrying owner `O` placed *before* the admin binds `O` wins permanently, and first-wins does not merely deny — it *awards*. A Budget carrying `O` and `O`'s `Parents` edges occupies `O`'s position in the family DAG and drinks the parent pool as Shared (cover walks parents first, `pkg/cover/cover.go:104-109`; every non-owner family tier classes Shared, `pkg/funding/funding.go:164-170`). Under my own P6 creation-time anchoring, the squat would even be time-anchored. First-wins converts a denial attack into family-position theft; symmetric poisoning fails toward denial. Rejected for the same reason my P5 trade-away gave: fail toward denial, away from theft (A:57).
- **Incumbency by accrual history** — adjudicate by which namespace holds leases stamped with `O`. Leases are controller-written facts, so the signal is authentic, but it makes identity a function of usage: fresh namespaces are unprotected, and an identity that follows consumption inverts the model (identity is declared, spending follows). A heuristic where the record demands a declaration. Rejected.
- **Namespace locality.** No asymmetry exists — each colliding Budget is local to its own namespace.

So within the inputs the ratified design allows, poisoning both was the only selection-free option, and Sol is right that this is detection, not selection. The scan lacked a legitimate asymmetry **because the ratified inputs carry no assertion of authority by anyone other than the claimant**. `Spec.Owner` is self-naming (api/v1/budget_types.go:30-31) and `Spec.Parents` is child-asserted — the *child's* Budget declares its parent edge with no existence or consent check (`budget_types.go:36`; `pkg/funding/funding.go:60-73`), which is exactly why the interior exemption became a cluster-wide switch the victim does not hold (`evaluate.go:220-222`, skip at :238-241; pinned at `pkg/funding/tenancy_r7_conflicts_test.go:130`).

**The delegation ruling supplies the missing asymmetry, and it fits inside the scan.** The ruling's structure is a chain of grants: root admins → managers → leads → researchers. Encode it as grantor-side edges: a Budget gains `Spec.Grants` — entries naming (grantee owner string, grantee namespace, per-flavor caps) — written on the *grantor's* Budget, which lives in the grantor's namespace, which namespaced RBAC already scopes. Legitimacy is then a trace: a Budget is legitimate iff its namespace is in the deploy-time root set (namespace identity being the one API-server-authenticated fact the design already leans on, R7:73-75), or a legitimate Budget carries a grant naming exactly its (owner, namespace). `deriveOwners` computes this by BFS from the roots — the same computational shape as `NewFamilyGraph`, still a pure scan, no new CRD, no webhook, no controller.

That trace is selection. Two Budgets carrying `O`: the victim's traces to a root, the squatter's does not — the squatter's Budget is ignored for identity and alarmed, and `OwnerOf(victim)` never moves. Sol's own predicate "changing any Budget outside namespace N cannot change `OwnerOf(N)`" (B:68) becomes provable for the scan in its corrected form: outside the *chain* of N — your grantors can affect you, which is delegation working, and is precisely "contained to what they were granted". Both-trace collisions (a grantor genuinely granted `O` twice, or two grantors did) remain symmetric and keep the poison-both fail-safe — but that residue is a within-chain error by someone holding authority over `O`, no longer foreign-inducible. Neither-trace: both ignored. And identity now survives leaf deletion: the grant persists on the grantor's Budget through a GitOps window that recreates the leaf's, so zero local Budgets means "known principal, currently no envelope" — Sol's separation (C:34-38), achieved grantor-side. W's job shrinks to whole-chain loss and genuine revocation; my P8 clause structure otherwise stands.

The invariants:

- **INV-OWNER-TRACES-TO-ROOT** (Evaluate postcondition): `OwnerOf(ns) == o ≠ ""` ⟹ a grant chain exists from a root-namespace Budget to `(o, ns)`, every link on a legitimate Budget.
- **INV-GRANT-LOCAL** (transition): a mutation touching only Budgets outside `chain(ns)` leaves `OwnerOf(ns)` and ns's loss timer unchanged. This is Sol's inducibility rail, made true for a scan.
- **INV-GRANT-CONSERVE** (steady): for every legitimate Budget P and flavor f, the sum of P's grants' caps(f) — hours and concurrency — does not exceed P's own envelope allocation(f). Checked as an Evaluate postcondition surfaced on the `Evaluation` plus an R26 alarm; the fail direction is loud defect, not silent clamp, and no overdraft is possible meanwhile because greedy fill still enforces each envelope's own caps (quota-semantics.md:35-37). Sub-grant *admission* enforcement beyond the predicate is a quota-semantics extension to spec separately, honestly noted.

Costs, plainly: `BudgetSpec` gains `Grants` (a CRD change — batch it into the lease-schema outage R7:473-475 already schedules); `Spec.Parents` demotes to derived-from-grants or validated-against-them; `Spec.Owner` survives as the family-axis *name* but loses self-naming authority; golden fixtures restructure again (the R7:428-443 exercise repeated); R18's runbook is rewritten around delegation; and §6's no-consent rationale — "both ends of every family edge are written by the same accountable party" (R7:288-289) — dies with its premise, because grantor and grantee are now different parties and the grant *is* the consent, asserted in the only direction that authenticates.

And the sentence I owe Sol: **on the central factual question — can self-naming `Budget.Spec.Owner` serve as the identity source — Sol was right and my position as filed was wrong.** Identity requires an assertion by someone other than the claimant. What I keep of my position is the computation (a deterministic scan over cluster state); what I give up is the input. Where we still differ is where the authority assertion lives — §4.

## 3. The partial-gang defect — conceded, repaired, one counter

Sol's charge is verified against the code. The three doors are real: the top-up path returns at `controllers/run_controller.go:1145` and the active-pods path at :1168-1171, so only a zero-hold activation reaches the terminal guard at :1216-1225. Demand reclaims them — Unfunded is the first cut (quota-semantics.md:108; evaluate.go:280-286) — but idleness never does, and the Run reads "assembling" indefinitely (:1136). On an idle cluster that is an immortal-class defect wearing different clothes. Conceded.

The repair adopts Sol's boundary because it is principled and already in the code: **work is a gang at or above `minRunnableGPUs`** (`run_controller.go:2732-2737` — `Malleable.MinTotalGPUs` or the full gang width). Below minimum at the same W that fails the reservation: unwind — leases closed through normal release, pods deleted, requeue whole. At or above minimum: it is work; a binding cause never closes it; replacement ranks within recorded width stay allowed. One clock, one number. This explicitly amends C-2's "pre-existing leases reclassify Unfunded and coast" (R7:588-592) with a carve-out for never-yet-runnable assemblies — Sol conceded its rule needs the same amendment (C:101), so it is now jointly proposed rather than a fork.

The counter I keep: **no new commitment object.** The durable commitment already exists as a pair the API server persists and authenticates: the Reservation, whose spec is immutable at the apiserver (`api/v1/reservation_types.go:16`) and names `RunRef` and `PayingEnvelope` (:33-39), and the Run spec's width (`TotalGPUs`, `Malleable.MinTotalGPUs`). Completion is bounded by that recorded width; growth while unbound is fresh admission and is refused — grow leases are already segregated by `Spec.Reason` (`run_controller.go:2804-2816`), which also answers my own A:190 cohort worry well enough for the rule (the residual reconstruction is R2 pt3's job, as the code says at :2806). Of Sol's proposed commitment fields, "binding revision" has no referent in a scan world (the grant's own `resourceVersion` serves if one is ever wanted) and "expiry" is W. A separate commitment record would persist what these two objects already persist.
